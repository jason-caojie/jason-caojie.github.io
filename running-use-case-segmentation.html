Category architecture study · August 2026 · Nike / adidas / ASICS / Saucony / New Balance / HOKA

Twelve running occasions. Four of them nobody is building for.

A MECE segmentation of running use cases, mapped against the current model matrices of the six major performance brands — built to show where the incumbent grid is dense, where it is duplicated, and where it is simply absent.

How the segmentation is cut

Unit of analysis

One run occasion — a single outing — not a runner type. Runners are not segments; the same person occupies five of these in a week and buys a different shoe for each.

Primary key

Surface class (road/hard · off-road · embedded) × the job the outing does (start · accumulate · protect · sharpen · compete).

Exhaustiveness

Every run a human takes lands in exactly one of the twelve. Nothing outside; nothing counted twice.

Tie-break rules

Assign to the occasion whose constraint dominates the shoe decision. An easy treadmill run is X1, not R2, because the belt changes the requirement.

What is deliberately not a segment: pronation/support need, foot width, body mass, climate, and price tier. These are orthogonal modifiers — they cut across all twelve occasions rather than partitioning them. Treating "stability" as a segment is the most common error in this category, and it is why brands end up with a stability shoe that has no occasion. They are handled separately in section 03.

03

The four modifiers that cut across all twelve

These are not segments — they are axes. Each one applies to every occasion above, which is why they belong in a fit/spec system rather than in the occasion grid. Getting this separation right is what stops a model matrix from doubling in size for no strategic reason.

ModifierHow it variesHow the six brands express it todayImplication for a new matrix
Support & pronation need Neutral → guided → structured. Overpronation is commonly cited at roughly 40–60% of runners, concentrated among beginners, higher-body-mass runners, flat arches and older runners. Nike Structure 26 / Structure Plus · adidas Supernova Solution & Adistar · ASICS GEL-KAYANO 33, GT-2000 15 · Saucony Guide 19, Tempus 3, Hurricane 25 · NB 860v15, Vongo v7 · HOKA Arahi 9, Gaviota 6. Note Nike now mirrors its neutral tiers — Structure 26 / Plus / Premium parallels Pegasus and Vomero. Build support as a variant of an occasion shoe, not a parallel line. Modern guidance rails and wide bases cost almost nothing in tooling versus a separate franchise.
Foot & body morphology Width (2A–6E), instep volume, toe-box shape, and body mass — a 95 kg runner compresses foam roughly twice as fast as the 68 kg tester most shoes are tuned around. New Balance leads on width breadth; ASICS and Saucony offer 2E/4E across core models; Nike is narrowest and offers extra-wide only on selected models. No major brand tunes midsole density by body mass. Density-tuned midsoles by weight band is unclaimed ground and matters most in R1, R2 and R4, where the buyer is heaviest and least served.
Environment Hot-humid · temperate · cold-wet · night/low-light · high-AQI. Determines upper, drainage, outsole compound and visibility. Handled almost entirely as a GTX or "waterproof" SKU aimed at cold-wet Northern Hemisphere winters. There is no equivalent engineering programme for hot-humid conditions. The single most under-served axis. Tropical and monsoon markets are large, growing, and currently sold temperate-market product with a different colourway.
Price tier Entry $100–130 · Core $140–180 · Premium trainer $200–260 · Race $250–500. The $50–100 band still holds the largest global unit share. All six now run a three-tier road architecture — Nike Icon/Plus/Premium, ASICS Novablast/Superblast/Megablast, HOKA Clifton/Skyflow/Skyward — which raises ASP but also multiplies near-duplicates. A new brand does not need three tiers per occasion. One correct shoe per occasion, with fit variants, is a defensible and far cheaper matrix.
04

Where the edge is — and isn't

Reading the coverage map commercially. Ranked by the ratio of demand to incumbent commitment, not by size alone.

Opening 01 · strongest

Hybrid fitness racing

A category that barely existed five years ago, now with a global race calendar and a shoe problem no running brand has solved: one shoe, two opposed biomechanical demands.

PUMA holds the official partnership and adidas has the only purpose-built alternative among the six. Nike, ASICS, Saucony, New Balance and HOKA field nothing, and their athletes are visibly improvising in road trainers.

Small now, defensible, and it recruits a customer who buys shoes on a 4–6 month cycle rather than 12.

Opening 02

Hot-humid climate engineering

Every one of the six designs its performance product for temperate conditions and then ships it globally. Weatherproofing means Gore-Tex for cold rain; nothing in the matrix addresses 32 °C at 85% humidity.

The functional brief is genuinely different: drainage over water resistance, foam that doesn't go soft in heat, anti-microbial linings, and outsole compound tuned for wet tile and painted road markings.

Cuts across R1–R5 and T1. A credible technical story that incumbents cannot copy without contradicting their own global platform.

Opening 03

Treadmill & indoor

Zero brands out of six name this occasion, yet it is a large and growing share of all running — driven by heat, air quality, safety and gym membership rather than preference.

The requirement genuinely differs from road: outsole mass can be removed, breathability becomes critical without airflow, and the run usually sits inside a gym session rather than standing alone.

Risk: buyers may not believe they need a specific shoe. Best played as a feature story inside R1/R2 rather than a standalone franchise.

Opening 04

Door-to-trail, done properly

The largest off-road population and the one most brands treat as a downmarket trail SKU. Most buyers here do not identify as trail runners; they simply have imperfect pavement.

Nike, ASICS and New Balance all serve it with adapted product. HOKA's Challenger is the only shoe of the six clearly built for the occasion, which is why it dominates it.

Moderate competition, real volume, and far cheaper to enter than technical mountain.

Avoid at launch · 01

Long-course race day

Six credible flagships, elite sponsorship as the entry ticket, PEBA-class tooling costs, and a buyer who chooses on marathon results they watched on television.

Highest halo, lowest odds. Earn the right to it in R5 first.

Avoid at launch · 02

Recovery & max cushion

Bondi, Nimbus, Triumph, More, Vomero — five near-identical answers to one question, all with a decade of brand equity and heavy discounting.

The most crowded square on the board. Nothing left to say here.
